Section dividers, at real scale.

Each candidate below sits at a genuine boundary from the live site. Judge them scrolling, not as thumbnails — a divider is a moment of pacing, and pacing does not survive a screenshot.

Tier 1 · safe Tier 2 · branded Tier 3 · signature
A · none — tonal flip
Baseline

Let the surface do it.

40 of your 83 boundaries flip between near-black and white. The tone change is already the strongest divider available; drawing a line across it adds a second announcement of something the eye has read. Applies to: hero→content, any .on-dark edge.

The flip is the divider.

Nothing was drawn between these two sections.

B · hairline, full bleed
Tier 1

A 1px rule, edge to edge.

The default. Honest, invisible when it should be, never wrong. Full-bleed reads as structural — it divides the page, not the column.

C · hairline, measure width
Tier 1

The same rule, held to the column.

Stopping the rule at the content measure is quieter and more editorial — it reads as a paragraph break rather than a page break. Better between two sections of the same argument.

D · numbered index
Tier 1 · recommended workhorse

Give the reader their position.

The rule carries a section number set in Fraunces at 300 weight — the .secno token already in pci.css and currently unused. This is the highest perceived quality per unit of risk on the list: it looks considered, it costs one pseudo-element, and it tells the reader where they are.

E · red seam
Tier 2 · recommended

The eyebrow dash, promoted.

A hairline with a short brand-red segment at the measure's left edge. It is the exact idiom your .eyebrow::before already uses 36 times, scaled up to structural duty — so it reads as native rather than added. This is the one I would ship site-wide.

F · crop ticks
Tier 2

Marks at the trim edge.

Short verticals where the measure meets the bleed. Reads as drawing-office registration, which suits the blueprint language on the project pages more than the institutional pages.

G · seam that draws on scroll
Tier 2 · motion

The same seam, arriving.

E, but the rule scales from the left as the boundary enters — driven by the .reveal observer already running, and flattened to a static rule under prefers-reduced-motion. Scroll this one back up to see it again.

H · the arc
Tier 3 · signature

Borrowed from the mark itself.

A single very large, very faint crescent — the same sweep as the C in your logo — straddling the boundary. It is the only option here that could not belong to another organisation. It is also the only one that will look cheap if it is used more than once on a page.

H · the arc, inverted
Tier 3 · signature

On black it earns more room.

Red at low opacity on near-black carries further than ink on white, so the dark placement can be larger and still stay subordinate to the copy.

I · inset step
Tier 2

The surface starts short.

The incoming surface is inset by one grid column, leaving a sliver of the previous one down the side. Architectural, and it needs a genuine surface change to work at all — useless on your 14 flat boundaries.

Considered and rejected

What I would not put on this site.

  • 01
    Diagonal / angled splits Fight a strict 12-column editorial grid, and date to a specific mid-2010s SaaS look. Every angle is a second, competing axis.
  • 02
    Wave and blob SVG dividers Read as consumer-app decoration. Wrong register for an institution that publishes reconciled figures.
  • 03
    Torn-paper and brush edges Texture implies craft-market or charity-appeal. Undercuts the convener positioning the copy works hard to establish.
  • 04
    Gradient fades between surfaces Muddies the black-and-white discipline the logo sets, and puts text over an indeterminate tone — the exact problem the hero scrim had to fix.
  • 05
    Full-width image bands as separators Costs real bandwidth on Ugandan mobile data for pure ornament.
