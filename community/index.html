How community work develops

The route is deliberate: listen first, define the work, organise responsibilities, then report what can be evidenced.

Step 01 · Listen

The community names the priority

Members, a school, a parish, an association or another local structure describes the need, who it affects and why it matters.

Step 02 · Define

Turn the need into clear work

The intended outcome, scope, responsibilities and evidence needed for approval are set out before the initiative is presented publicly.

Step 03 · Organise

Bring resources and partners together

The community identifies what it can contribute and where technical, public, philanthropic or business support may be appropriate.

Step 04 · Deliver and report

Keep the record connected to the work

Approved initiatives publish their status, verified progress and next responsibility in language the community and partners can understand.

What counts as a community project

Pamodzi considers work that can be defined, verified and reported on responsibly. If your initiative fits the marks below, tell us about it.

  • 01A defined piece of workA clear scope with an end — something that can be called finished, not an open-ended fund.
  • 02An authorised account to receive fundsMoney goes directly to a formally authorised account. Pamodzi and the campaign team never receive or hold a donation.
  • 03Something verifiableBeneficiary, need and progress can be confirmed and reported on — so only reconciled figures are ever published.
  • 04A community behind itWork shaped and carried by the people it is intended to serve.
