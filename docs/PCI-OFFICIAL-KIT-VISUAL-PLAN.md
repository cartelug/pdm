## 3. Visual direction

Create the feeling of one carefully photographed East African campaign: a person making a real physical effort, a community with agency, and a specific educational destination. Shyaka's gait makes the walk immediately understandable; children's engagement makes its purpose clear; the real main block makes the intended destination recognisable.

Use believable documentary photography, quiet cinematic composition, natural skin, tactile cotton and nylon, coherent sunlight, restrained depth and real contact shadows. Express hope through activity and relationships. Children read, discuss, build and play; they are not decorative props. Community members participate, rather than forming an anonymous cheering wall in every scene.

Retain PCI's existing crimson, warm paper and dark ink. Preserve the Walk page's established blue accents. The new yellow accents come from the actual shirt/bag print, rather than adding a competing website colour system. A warm highlight may travel through the images as a visual motif, but must behave as sunlight rather than a neon magical trail.

**One dominant action per scene.** Usually Shyaka, a learner interaction, or the main building. Supporting details create depth without competing for attention. No giant head, oversized logos, pasted collage borders, invented monuments, repeated duplicate faces, constant flying particles, or excessive blur.

## 4. Identity, wardrobe and architecture locks

1. Use the real Shyaka portrait in every generation that shows his face, together with a previously accepted anchor image after the first anchor exists. Match his facial geometry, beard, hair/headwrap and skin tone. Do not substitute a look-alike.
2. Keep an anatomically plausible adult head-to-body relationship, shoulders, neck, limbs and hands. Approximately 7–8 head lengths can be a proportion check for a neutral full-body pose; it is not a claimed measurement of Shyaka's height. Do not transfer Neeza's height or build to him.
3. Walking means a believable weight transfer, alternating arm swing, grounded leading/trailing feet and sensible shoulder loading. No running, floating, duplicated limbs or both feet inexplicably suspended.
4. The backpack must be the uploaded practical black bag: correct general proportions, carry handles, curved top, zip seams, front compartments and ordered print areas. Its straps follow his shoulders and torso; it casts a matching shadow. Do not put a second bag on him.
5. Keep the uploaded black shirt with natural cotton folds and plausible fit. Preserve the observed print design; do not make fabric logos float, glow, multiply or move between shots. Where exact brand legibility matters, show the original reference photograph or approved artwork rather than passing off invented lettering as accurate.
6. Use the actual main block. Preserve its blue framing, cream wall panels, repeated balcony rails, window rhythm, pitched roof and brick stair tower. Avoid architectural views the reference cannot support. Prefer the supplied three-quarter view and modest variations rather than fabricating the unseen rear.
7. Keep the shirt's Nairobi → Kagando, 1,300 km and 1,650,000 Steps as reference print, not proof of distance already walked. Live distances and current totals must come from approved operations data.
8. Build each scene around a small cast of distinct, plausible people. Preserve age-appropriate proportions and natural expressions. No school badges, identifiable real children or named beneficiaries are inferred from a generated face.
9. Generated arrival, welcome and future-learning scenes are campaign illustrations. They must never be published as a completed arrival, dated field report, donor acknowledgement or photograph documenting a real event.
